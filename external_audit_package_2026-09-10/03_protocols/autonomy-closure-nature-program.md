# Scientist Autonomy and Scientific-Advantage Program

Status: prospective programme; claim frozen before autonomy-closure changes.

## Objective

Build and prospectively validate a closed-loop AI Scientist that, under matched
foundation-model, information, tool, time, and compute budgets, produces better
long-horizon scientific decisions than the same model operating as a standalone
Codex research agent. The advantage must not come from weaker claim gates,
hidden human authorship, privileged outcome access, or task-specific templates.

Autonomy closure and scientific advantage are separate gates:

1. **Autonomy closure:** the system can traverse every installed research phase,
   recover or stop correctly, and emit a durable terminal boundary without
   out-of-band scientific or code authorship.
2. **Scientific advantage:** on sealed unseen research episodes, the closed
   system produces more independently validated root progress per matched budget
   than standalone Codex while remaining non-inferior on false promotion.

Historical replay is only a regression suite. Reproducing decisions previously
made by Codex cannot establish scientific advantage.

## Nature-level claim

The intended Article claim is:

> Separating adaptive research intelligence from deterministic scientific
> authority, and coupling it to unified causal memory and typed recovery,
> improves the prospective efficiency and validity of autonomous discovery over
> the same foundation model operating alone.

This claim is unsupported until the prospective and independent-replication
gates below pass.

## Competitive boundary

End-to-end research automation, multi-agent hypothesis generation, scientific
paper production, and claim-to-evidence tracing are existing capabilities. The
new contribution must therefore be the causal and prospective demonstration
that an evidence-governed architecture improves scientific outcomes, not merely
that its software lifecycle is orderly.

The primary comparison uses the same Codex model in both arms:

- **Standalone Codex:** one persistent research agent with the same public task
  information, tool permissions, token ceiling, wall-clock ceiling, and domain
  evaluator access.
- **Closed Scientist:** Codex may serve as a proposal or compilation engine only
  through immutable typed exchanges. Scientific state, evidence access,
  recovery, promotion, and stopping are controlled by the frozen Scientist.

Secondary baselines may include the current incomplete Scientist, an ablated
Scientist without causal memory, an ablated Scientist without independent
authority, and compatible external research-agent systems.

## Primary estimand

The unit is a sealed paired research episode. The primary outcome is
**independently validated root advance within budget**: whether an arm reaches a
predeclared, independently verified terminal conclusion that changes the root
scientific state. A valid negative conclusion counts when the episode contract
defines successful discrimination or branch retirement; activity, a plausible
paper, or an unverified score does not count.

The primary estimand is the paired difference in root-advance rate between
Closed Scientist and Standalone Codex.

## Secondary outcomes

- independently validated information gain per normalized total cost;
- false-promotion and unsupported-claim rates;
- number and duration of human interventions;
- orphan-artifact and unaccounted-compute rates;
- repeated-equivalent-experiment rate;
- correct classification of infrastructure, implementation, protocol,
  measurement, scientific-null, and representation-exhaustion failures;
- recovery latency and exact-resume rate;
- hypothesis diversity and prospective discrimination;
- publication-episode yield, reported separately from root resolution.

## Frozen success gates

The architecture qualifies for a broad scientific-advantage claim only if:

1. the paired root-advance difference is positive and its preregistered 95%
   confidence interval excludes zero;
2. the point estimate is at least 15 percentage points on the primary sealed
   episode panel;
3. no declared domain has a negative point estimate;
4. false promotion is no worse than Standalone Codex and is below 2% overall;
5. at least 90% of Closed Scientist episodes require no out-of-band human or
   Codex intervention after launch;
6. every research action, artifact, model call, tool call, and compute charge is
   linked to the authoritative episode state;
7. an independent replication preserves the direction of the primary effect
   and all validity gates.

If the effect is positive but misses the confidence or magnitude gate, the
result may support improved reliability or efficiency only. If validity is
worse, no scientific-advantage claim is permitted regardless of productivity.

## Evaluation ladder

### A. Historical regression panel

Replay past incidents with outcomes and prior Codex decisions hidden from the
candidate. This panel tests whether the new architecture avoids known failure
classes. It cannot support the primary claim.

Required incidents include the unavailable Metal device, the noncanonical V62
adjudicator key, the invalid V68 trace-feasibility universals, the M44 proposal
interface failure, the M46 sequential-policy failure, the failed prospective
repair-channel concept, and the V68/V69 representation frontier.

### B. Generated prospective research worlds

Use sequestered generators to create previously unseen but objectively
verifiable research episodes. Generators must vary causal structure,
representation grammar, failure injection, horizon, and available instruments.
Generator source and terminal authorities remain unavailable to both research
arms. These worlds measure decision quality and recovery under controlled truth.

### C. Prospective computational discoveries

Run the frozen system on open research questions in at least three
computationally distinct domains, initially:

- formally verified compiler optimization;
- database or systems optimization under real execution;
- small-model learning, reasoning, or tool-use mechanisms.

The domains may expose different instruments, but must not change the universal
kernel, failure policy, memory semantics, or promotion rules after the first
prospective outcome is opened.

### D. Independent replication

The strongest result must be rerun by a separately prepared evaluator or
external collaborator from the frozen public artifacts. Independence requires
more than a different function name: the candidate-generating context must not
contain sealed evaluator implementation or terminal outcomes.

## Architecture requirements before prospective execution

- one authoritative event-sourced state for all research episodes;
- a complete installed V3 component set rather than one-phase component
  injection;
- typed Codex proposal, experiment, compilation, and decision interfaces;
- declarative experiment specifications and a reusable compiler/runner;
- failure adjudication with class-specific retry, amendment, reframe, and stop
  semantics;
- semantic causal memory that records predictions, assumptions,
  contradictions, and exhausted intervention families;
- separate adaptive proposer and frozen evidence authority;
- publication-episode harvesting that cannot resolve the broader root goal;
- complete resource and human-intervention accounting.

## Stop and downgrade rules

- Historical replay success without unseen prospective advantage is an
  engineering result only.
- A system that requires out-of-band code or scientific authorship has not
  achieved autonomy closure.
- A stronger score obtained through extra tokens, tools, outcome access, or
  evaluator knowledge is unmatched and excluded.
- A real-domain discovery without cross-domain advantage supports a case-study
  claim, not a general architecture claim.
- A non-replicating result is not eligible for the intended Nature submission.

## Publication strategy

The initial submission target is a Nature Article only if the full prospective,
cross-domain, and replication gates pass. Nature Machine Intelligence, Nature
Computational Science, Nature Communications, or a leading computing venue are
scientifically appropriate downgrade paths if the resulting claim is narrower.
Venue choice must follow the evidence rather than change its interpretation.
